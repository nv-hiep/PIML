**4. Preliminary results for Physics-Informed Inference**:\
<img width="2493" height="1140" alt="GASKAP_GALAH_los" src="https://github.com/nv-hiep/pii/blob/main/figs/eg00.png" />
Figure 3: Physics-informed inference framework for inferring gas temperature from GASKAP emission observations. Left: Observed emission spectrum (blue) as the input, reconstructed spectrum (red). Middle: Observed gas temperature spectrum (blue) and inferred temperature (red); significant fluctuation of observed temperatures below −10 kms−1 and above 20 kms−1 rises from the amplified noise in emission and absorption measurements. Right: Observed absorption spectrum (blue) and inferred optical depth (red). Gray bands indicate the velocity range of detected absorption (hence reliable measured gas temperatures), cyan envelopes represent presentative inference uncertainties. \

**5. Preliminary results for HISA detectors**:\
<img width="2493" height="1140" alt="GASKAP_GALAH_los" src="https://github.com/nv-hiep/pii/blob/main/figs/RC_HISA_with_activation.png" />
Figure 1: Upper panel: Example of a detected HISA feature (red dotted line) in an HI emission spectrum observed towards the Riegel-Crutcher cloud (distance of ~125 pc or ~3.86 × 10<sup>15</sup> km from the Sun). Lower panel: Activation (or saliency) spectra generated by our HISA detector, raw activation spectrum in grey, smoothed activation spectrum in blue, and softmax activation in red. The velocity at the maximum activation value determines the central velocity of the detected HISA feature. \

<img width="2493" height="1140" alt="GASKAP_GALAH_los" src="https://github.com/nv-hiep/pii/blob/main/figs/RC_HISA_velocity_map.png" />
Figure 2: HISA velocities identified by our conceptual HISA detector in the Riegel-Crutch cloud (at a distance of ~125 pc or ~3.86 × 10<sup>15</sup> km from the Sun). The colorbar represents the HISA velocity values. The spatial coherence of the HISA velocities is illustrated. The gray background shows the total emission at 5.5 km s<sup>-1</sup>. The direction towards the Milky Way center is at the center of the Figure, (longitude, latitude) = (0<sup>o</sup>, 0<sup>o</sup>). \
